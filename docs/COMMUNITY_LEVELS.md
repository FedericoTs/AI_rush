# The Lab — Community Levels

Players describe a level. We build the good ones. Their handle goes on it,
in-game and on every share card that mentions it, forever.

## 1. Why this exists

Three reasons, in order of importance:

1. **Content supply.** 36 levels is roughly 7 runs before repetition becomes
   noticeable. A game like this dies of content starvation. Crowdsourcing the
   *ideas* — the expensive, creative part — while we keep the *implementation*
   is the only sustainable model at our size.
2. **A second share loop that isn't about score.** "They built my level" is a
   far stronger post than "I scored 4,150", and it comes from people who are
   already invested.
3. **It closes the joke.** A game about AI-generated interfaces, where the
   levels are actually generated by humans being cruel to each other, is a
   better joke than the premise alone.

## 2. The submission flow

Entry points: the post-run share screen (*"You think you can do worse?"*), and
`/lab` directly.

**The form is completely normal.** Clean, accessible, well-labelled, generous
touch targets, working autofill, no tricks. This is deliberate and it is a bit:
a small line under the heading reads *"We made this one usable on purpose so
you'd actually finish it."* It is also genuinely the right call — friction here
costs us the content pipeline.

Fields:

| Field | Constraint | Prompt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `title` | 3–80 chars | "What does the interface call itself?" |
| `parodies` | 3–120 chars | "What normal UI is it pretending to be?" |
| `mechanic` | 20–1200 chars | "What does it do to the player? Be specific about what makes it survivable — every level must be beatable." |
| `inputs` | multi-select | touch, tilt, mic, camera, keyboard, mouse, vibration, sound |
| `x_handle` | required | "Where do we credit you?" |

The `mechanic` prompt explicitly asks for the honest solve. Submissions that
describe only the cruelty and not the escape are the most common failure mode,
and asking directly fixes most of it.

## 3. Voting and the gallery

`/lab` shows approved submissions as cards with the author's handle, sortable by
**Top**, **New**, and **Shipped**.

- One vote per submission per browser fingerprint + IP hash (`submission_votes`,
  see `BACKEND.md`).
- Votes are a **signal, not a mandate.** A 400-vote idea that's mechanically
  identical to Level 12 doesn't get built. We say so publicly on the card
  (`status: not shipping — too close to L12`), because silently ignoring the
  top-voted idea is how community programs lose trust.
- Shipped submissions get a `SHIPPED` stamp and link to the level.

## 4. Credit

When a community level appears in a run:

- A small persistent byline in the level chrome: `level by @handle`
- The level's entry in `LEVELS.md` and the in-game level index carries the
  credit
- **The share card names them** if that level was the cause of death:
  `CAUSE OF DEATH: "Infinite Zoom Consent" — a level by @someone`

That last one is the whole incentive. Getting your handle into thousands of
strangers' share cards is worth more than any prize we could offer.

## 5. Moderation

Manual, human, and small — appropriate to the actual volume.

- Submissions are `pending` and **invisible** until a human approves them.
  There is no auto-publish tier.
- Submitted text is stored as text and rendered as text. **Never** interpreted
  as HTML, never passed to a code generator, never used to construct a prompt
  that produces shipped code. A submission is a design brief a human reads.
  This is the security boundary and it is not negotiable for convenience.
- Rejection reasons are public on the card (`too close to L12`, `not
  implementable`, `requires hardware we can't assume`, `off-topic`).
- Handles are validated as plausible X handles (`^[A-Za-z0-9_]{1,15}$`) but
  **not verified**. A `verified` boolean exists in the schema, unused, for when
  X OAuth is added.
- Rate limit: 3 submissions per IP hash per day.
- A takedown path (`/api/lab/remove`) for anyone impersonated.

## 6. Cadence

Sustainable, not ambitious:

- **Weekly:** triage the queue, approve/reject with public reasons.
- **Every 2 weeks:** ship 1–2 community levels as a named drop
  (`Community Drop 03`), announced from the game's X account with the authors
  tagged. Tagged authors post about it. That is the loop.
- **Monthly:** retire the two worst-performing levels by telemetry
  (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §8) so the deck stays sharp rather than merely large.

## 7. Guardrails on what we build

A community level ships only if it satisfies the design pillars in
`GAME_DESIGN.md` — particularly:

- **P1:** it must look like a real UI for the first 1.5 seconds
- **P2:** the failure must be nameable in one sentence
- **P3:** honest solve within par, and failing must be cheap
- **P5 corollary:** it must have a degraded path for missing sensors

If an idea is great but violates P3 (fun but 90 seconds long), we shorten it
and say so on the card, crediting the author for the idea rather than the exact
spec. Transparency about edits is cheaper than the argument that follows a
silent rewrite.
